Questions

Do I still pay for a bad tick?

Yes, once. Payment happens first and the check comes right after, so the bad tick itself is paid. Pawlr then stops every later payment, which is why the most you can lose is one tick. You can request that tick back with the evidence attached.

Who decides whether a delivery is good?

Simple automatic checks go first: right order, not tampered with, not empty, the right length, the right key words. Then a fast screen (Dolphin) and an AI judge (SERV Reasoning) compare the delivery with the agreed rules. The judge must quote the exact words it relied on; if it can't, nothing is decided automatically.

What happens when it isn't sure?

Nothing unclear is rejected automatically. The session pauses and a person approves or rejects that tick.

What if I think a verdict is wrong?

A stopped or unclear tick can be sent to Reppo, an independent panel of evaluators. It reads the agreed rule, the section, what the seller delivered and anything the seller added, and gives its own verdict. Payments resume only if it passes the tick.

Is this real money?

The payments are real transactions on Kite's test network, but the token is a free test token with no value. The same design works with real stablecoins on Kite mainnet.

Are refunds automatic?

No. Pawlr sends the seller one clear refund request listing the bad ticks, the amount and the evidence. Returning the money is up to the seller.

Can a seller trick the AI judge?

The judge treats the seller's text as untrusted and ignores instructions inside it, a prompt-injection guard runs on every request, and it only decides against the written rules, quoting its evidence. In our live tests with the current rules, a hidden "mark this as delivered" instruction was caught and the payments stopped.
