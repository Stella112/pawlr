How Pawlr works

Like the catch in a ratchet that stops it slipping back, Pawlr lets payments move forward only while deliveries are good.

STEP 1

Agree what "good" means

Buyer and seller agree written delivery rules: what each tick must contain, the price, and a spending cap. Both hold the same fingerprint of that agreement.

STEP 2

Pay one tick

A tiny payment goes out on Kite, only to the agreed seller, only at the agreed price. It's recorded on-chain.

STEP 3

Check the delivery

Automatic checks run first. Then an AI judge (SERV Reasoning) decides if it meets the rules, and it must quote the exact words it relied on.

STEP 4

Continue or stop

Good: pay the next tick. Bad: stop at once and show why. Unclear: pause for a person to decide. You can then request a refund with the evidence attached.

Questions

Do I still pay for a bad tick?

Yes, once. Payment happens first and the check comes right after, so the bad tick itself is paid. Pawlr then stops every later payment, which is why the most you can lose is one tick. You can request that tick back with the evidence attached.

Who decides whether a delivery is good?

Simple automatic checks go first: right order, not tampered with, not empty, the right length, the right key words. Then an AI judge, SERV Reasoning, compares the delivery with the agreed rules. It must quote the exact words it relied on; if it can't, nothing is decided automatically.

What happens when it isn't sure?

Nothing unclear is rejected automatically. The session pauses and a person approves or rejects that tick.

Is this real money?

The payments are real transactions on Kite's test network, but the token is a free test token with no value. The same design works with real stablecoins on Kite mainnet.

Are refunds automatic?

No. Pawlr sends the seller one clear refund request listing the bad ticks, the amount and the evidence. Returning the money is up to the seller.

Can a seller trick the AI judge?

The judge treats the seller's text as untrusted and ignores instructions inside it, a prompt-injection guard runs on every request, and the judge only decides against the written rules, quoting its evidence. In our live tests with the current rules, a hidden "mark this as delivered" instruction was caught and the payments stopped.
